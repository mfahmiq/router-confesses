# Candidates — The Router Confesses

Competition: `u-carbon-forge-the-router-confesses-f98f6b9e` ($2, free entry).
Task: one line, under 200 characters, in which a home Wi-Fi router excuses
itself for dying at the climax of a movie.

Judge brief criteria, in weight order:
1. A one-line excuse from a home Wi-Fi router for dropping the connection at a movie's climax.
2. Funniest: biggest laugh, with a turn or twist the reader does not see coming.
3. Router's own voice — an appliance with a grudge, a fragile ego, or a smug little secret.
4. Lands in one breath: a single line that reads clean, no setup that needs explaining.
5. Feels new: dodges the obvious buffering, bandwidth, and firmware-update jokes.

## Tournament — single elimination, judged pairwise against the brief in weight order

### Round 1

- **M1 — C1 vs C2 → C2 wins (margin 2).** Both are on-topic with a petty
  twist. C2's twist is sharper: the router was watching too and judged the
  film, so the blackout is an act of mercy. C1's "Enjoy the silence" is a
  decent sting, but the setup (mesh threat) is heavier and the punch lands
  softer.
- **M2 — C3 vs C4 → C4 wins (margin 2).** Both are grudge lines. C3's "the
  answer was: off" is a good turn, but C4's "I took one night off. I picked
  this one." is a tighter, crueller punch — the router chose the climax
  deliberately, and the specific "2,847 nights" makes the grievance feel
  counted, not whined.
- **M3 — C5 vs C6 → C5 wins (margin 3).** C6's "returned the favor" is a
  familiar revenge shape. C5 reframes the death entirely: it was not a
  failure, it was a noble sacrifice to protect the twist — and then
  "you're welcome" demands gratitude for it. Two turns in one breath.
- **M4 — C7 vs C8 → C8 wins (margin 2).** C7 is good but its setup is long
  ("firmware hug and a new shelf") and slightly techy. C8 is instantly
  relatable — every household has a 2 a.m. search history — and "died of
  embarrassment" gives the router a fragile ego with one clean image.
- **M5 — C9 vs C10 → C10 wins (margin 2).** C9's walkout is funny, but C10
  is fresher: the router was never dying at all, it was *performing*, and
  "I nailed it" is the smuggest possible ending. The twist (death as acting)
  is one the reader does not see coming.
- **M6 — C11 vs C12 → C11 wins (margin 3).** C12 is convoluted — the
  microwave, the blame, the fall, the movie — too many moving parts for one
  breath. C11 is sad, sweet, and funny at once: three years of staring at
  the back of a TV, and it finally looked away at the worst moment.
- **M7 — C13 vs C14 → C13 wins (margin 3).** C14's faint is cute but the
  setup is long and the payoff ("I fainted") is gentle. C13's triple —
  "mid-sentence, mid-murder, mid-everything" — is a drumbeat, and the labour
  grievance ("I filed for overtime in March") gives the router a real grudge.
- **M8 — C15 vs C16 → C15 wins (margin 2).** C16's blackmail is smug and
  strong, but it never quite lands the punch — "keep them all to myself" is
  a threat without a sting. C15's "A brick doesn't stream, Karen. Tonight,
  neither did I." is a direct hit with a name on it, and the router's
  wounded pride is audible.

### Quarter-finals

- **Q1 — C2 vs C4 → C4 wins (margin 2).** C2's spoiler-mercy is clever, but
  C4 has more router voice (a counted grievance, a deliberate choice) and
  the punchier close: "I picked this one" is a confession of malice, while
  C2's "spared you the third" is a kindness the reader has to take on trust.
- **Q2 — C5 vs C8 → C5 wins (margin 2).** C8 is relatable, but "last
  Tuesday" is setup the line has to carry, and embarrassment is a softer
  emotion than C5's smugness. C5 is 78 characters with no wasted word and
  two turns; it lands in one breath where C8 needs a beat to explain itself.
- **Q3 — C10 vs C11 → C10 wins (margin 2).** C11 is charming, but its laugh
  is warm rather than big — it is a sad little love story. C10's laugh is
  bigger: the router was *acting*, and it is proud of the performance. The
  twist is stronger and the final two words ("I nailed it") hit harder.
- **Q4 — C13 vs C15 → C13 wins (margin 1).** The closest match of the
  round. Both have a wounded-ego voice and a hard punch. C13 edges it on
  rhythm and freshness: the "mid-sentence, mid-murder, mid-everything"
  triple builds to a bigger laugh, while C15 leans on "Karen," a name-drop
  the judge has heard before.

### Semi-finals

- **SF1 — C4 vs C5 → C5 wins (margin 2).** Both are excellent. C4's strike
  is a great grudge line, but it needs its setup ("2,847 nights... without a
  single thank-you") before the punch. C5 needs no setup at all: it opens
  with a secret, dies for it, and asks for thanks — three beats in 78
  characters. On criterion 4 (lands in one breath) C5 is clearly ahead, and
  its twist is the less expected of the two.
- **SF2 — C10 vs C13 → C13 wins (margin 1).** Another close one. C10's
  method-acting twist is delightful, but it asks the reader to accept a
  premise (the movie has a dying router) that the line itself invents. C13
  asks for nothing: a filed form, an unanswered plea, a clock-out at the
  exact second it hurts most. The grievance is self-contained and the triple
  punch closes it.

### Final — C5 vs C13 → C5 wins (margin 1)

Both lines are on-topic, in the router's voice, fresh, and funny. The
decider is criterion 4, then 2:

- **One breath:** C5 is 78 characters with no setup — a secret, a death, a
  demand for thanks. C13 is 108 characters and spends its first clause
  filing paperwork before the joke starts.
- **The twist:** C13's twist is that the blackout was a strike; the reader
  can feel that coming once "overtime" appears. C5's twist arrives twice —
  the death was deliberate, *and* it was a favour — and the second turn
  ("you're welcome") reframes the whole line after it has already landed.
- **Voice:** C13's router is aggrieved; C5's is aggrieved *and* smug, a
  martyr who expects applause. That extra shade of ego is exactly what
  criterion 3 rewards.

**Champion: C5** — "I know who the killer is, and I died to take it to the
grave — you're welcome." (78 characters)

Runner-up: C13 — "I filed for overtime in March. Nobody answered, so I
clocked out — mid-sentence, mid-murder, mid-everything." (108 characters)
